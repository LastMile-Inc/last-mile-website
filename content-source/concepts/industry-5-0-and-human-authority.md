# Industry 5.0 and Human Authority

## Canonical position

Industry 5.0 is a human-centric, sustainable, and resilient industrial vision, not a new automation version number. Last Mile supports it by keeping authority explicit, making degraded states and resilience visible, and verifying resource and operating outcomes.

## Publication-ready article

### More automation is not the same as more industrial intelligence

Industry 4.0 helped focus industry on connectivity, cyber-physical systems, data, and automation. The European Commission's Industry 5.0 framing broadens the objective around three priorities: human-centricity, sustainability, and resilience.

That framing matters for Last Mile because operational intelligence is not simply the fastest automated response. A system must show who owns the condition, what authority is retained, what evidence supports the recommendation, how the response affects operating resilience, and whether the intended physical result occurred.

Last Mile's AUTO, ASSIST, and HUMAN AUTHORITY modes make that boundary visible. Routine digital correlation, notification, and case creation can occur automatically. Evidence and recommended action can be assembled for an operator. Physical work, safety decisions, and control changes remain under customer authority where policy requires it.

Resilience also needs a better measure than ticket volume. Reduced redundancy, unavailable standby capacity, failed recovery, insufficient return data, and recurrence should remain visible even when the alarm console looks quiet. Sustainability requires the same discipline: compressed-air flow or aeration energy should be measured against operating context and verified after intervention, not converted into a generic claim.

The Last Mile position is therefore practical Industry 5.0: use technology to strengthen people, resilience, and resource outcomes while retaining accountability for the physical world.

## What this looks like in an operating response

At wastewater station `LS-07`, AUTO time-aligns level, rate, current, speed, flow, and standby evidence. ASSIST presents a degraded-capacity Condition and the approved response to Luis Ortega. Collection Systems retains isolation, LOTO, standby-pump, and field-work authority. Recovery requires three complete drawdown cycles with valid flow, current, and level evidence; faster automation does not replace human accountability or resilience criteria.
